2026-04-25 — Reading note: Kuribayashi, Warstadt, Oseki & Wilcox (2026), "Dual Alignment Between Language Model Layers and Human Sentence Processing" (arXiv 2604.18563v1). Logit-lens layer-wise surprisal across 19 LMs (GPT-2, OPT, Pythia) tested on Huang et al. (2024) syntactic ambiguity reading-time data. Earlier layers' surprisal best models naturalistic reading; later layers' surprisal best models reading-time slowdowns at syntactic ambiguities (MVRR, NPS, NPZ, RC, Attachment). Effect strengthens with model scale. They call this "dual alignment" and explicitly conclude that LM surprisal is not "a single unified predictor" of human sentence processing; you have to look at parts of the LM as models of sub-processes. This is the projection-purpose / cluster reading of LM cognitive modeling, arrived at by entirely different (mechanistic-interpretability) means. Citable as empirical support for treating LMs as a bundle of dissociable predictors rather than a homogeneous system. When next touching this paper, consider citing alongside Yetman in the section that argues against monolithic readings of "what LMs do."

2026-07-14 — Reading note: Iaia and Tavano (2026), "Prior knowledge reveals two computational regimes for syntactic processing in the human brain" (bioRxiv v1, DOI `10.64898/2026.07.11.737945`; source: `/Users/brettreynolds/Downloads/2026.07.11.737945v1.full.md`). In naturalistic audiobook MEG, Brown-Corpus first-order transition probabilities over parser-derived features improved decoding of memory-related unresolved syntactic structure mainly at lags −1 and −2, while integration-related structural-resolution features showed no comparable benefit and were generally stronger at word offset. At the next preprint refresh, consider this after von der Malsburg and Padó as a human-side reminder that a shared description as "prediction," or neural alignment with model predictions, does not establish shared processing constraints. Cautions: this is a human-only correlational decoding study, not an LLM comparison; "prior knowledge" is a corpus proxy rather than measured participant knowledge; independent lag models do not measure accumulated context; the onset/offset contrast is graded; and the source's syntactic "integration" is narrower than this paper's cognitive-integration cluster. Do not generalize the one-to-two-lag result to human context use generally or treat the theory-defined decoding dissociation as two identified causal neural systems. The evidence is a non-peer-reviewed v1 preprint based on 25 English listeners and four stories.

2026-07-14 — Reading note: [Dobriban (2026), “The Benjamini–Hochberg Procedure Can Fail to Control the FDR for Correlated Two-Sided Gaussian Tests”](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/bh.pdf). The author's report that GPT-5.6 Pro found the counterexample, followed by an analytic reduction, outward-rounded certificate, simulation, and human audit, is a useful functional-capacity case: it licenses at most the local claim that this human-model workflow produced a certifiable result on this task. At the next revision, consider it in the capacity/integration distinction rather than as a headline about general intelligence. Cautions: a single author-reported, post-selected episode does not establish broad reasoning ability, autonomy, cognitive integration, mechanism, or a controlled improvement over GPT-5.5; the post and manuscript use slightly different model labels; and human problem formulation, checking, and exposition are part of the causal workflow. A local audit replayed the standalone certificate but did not independently audit the analytic reduction, while the current [public bundle](https://github.com/dobriban/BH) fails its advertised full verifier because its manifest and file layout have diverged. The packaging defect neither refutes the theorem nor strengthens the model-level attribution.
