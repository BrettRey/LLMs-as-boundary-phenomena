**Why this project cares.** The article frames the LRM debate as exactly the binary this paper
diagnoses: "genuinely reasoning" versus Vallor's "meta-mimicry" (spelled "Valor" in the
capture). Mitchell then undercuts the binary herself: "'Reasoning' is an umbrella term for many
different types of cognitive problem-solving processes," listing memorized steps, heuristics,
analogy, and deduction ~-- a property-cluster claim about a cognitive predicate, in a *Science*
perspective, from the field's most prominent LLM skeptic. Usable two ways: as evidence that
the essentialist framing persists even among careful commentators (she still poses the
either/or before dissolving it), and as independent support for treating cognitive predicates
as overloaded cluster terms. Her interpretability caveat (chains of thought may not be faithful
to internal processing) also feeds the paper's neuroscience-vs-functional projection split:
the behavioral trace and the mechanism can dissociate.
