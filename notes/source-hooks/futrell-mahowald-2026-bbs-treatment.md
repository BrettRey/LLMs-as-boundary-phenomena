language models. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 49, e198: 1–80. doi:10.1017/S0140525X2510112X.

**Why this project cares.** This paper is a comment on Nefdt (2026). Nefdt has himself published a
commentary, with Ladyman, inside this treatment (BBS 49 e219, at p. 62), so the conversation Brett
is entering already contains its subject as a participant. Reading the two together shows what
Nefdt commits to in print when responding to a positive account of LMs, which is a different and
more constrained setting than his solo work.

**What it may support or challenge.** F&M argue that LMs "contribute to fundamental questions about
linguistic structure, language processing, and learning" and act as "model systems and working
proofs of concept for gradient, usage-based approaches," without replacing structure and theory.
That is a positive account against which a boundary-phenomena reading can be positioned. Whether
boundary status is a rival to, or a refinement of, "model system and proof of concept" is the
question worth putting directly.

**The treatment is also an argument map.** Twenty-five commentaries: Ambridge, Lieven, Kenny Smith,
Rawski, Lidz & Howitt, Lampinen, Lupyan, Millière, Nevins, Colin Phillips, Nefdt & Ladyman, Resnik,
Newport, Tripp and others, plus an authors' response at p. 69. Millière and Lupyan are the likeliest
direct interlocutors for this project's framing. Before claiming a position is unoccupied, check it
here.
