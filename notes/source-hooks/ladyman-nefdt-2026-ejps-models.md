**Source:** Ladyman, J., & Nefdt, R. M. 2026. Are AI language models scientific models of
language? *EJPS*, forthcoming (per Nefdt's publications page 2026-08-14; no DOI yet). Preprint
PhilSci-Archive 27973, on disk with extraction. Central note:
`literature/ladyman-nefdt-2026-ai-models-of-language.notes.md`.

**Why this project cares.** The third Nefdt 2026 statement (solo manuscript; BBS commentary
with Ladyman; now this), and the most dialectically useful. They argue LLMs are scientific
models that capture language's modal structure without its causal structure, through three
distinct conduits (corpus models; idealized learning models; models of language itself), with
model goodness explicitly purpose-relative (their thesis 7) and modal-structure representation
explicitly tied to projectibility (§4: "models can be taken to represent modal structure
accurately in so far as they are projectible"). That is the purpose-relative, projectibility-
first machinery this paper accuses the solo 2×2 of abandoning. The reply can now cite Nefdt's
own co-authored work as supplying the framework: the three conduits are three projections,
each with its own verdict, which is the tomato move in their vocabulary.

**Where it pushes back.** Their framework is modal-structure realism (ontic structural
realism), not cluster kinds: the purpose-relativity attaches to *models* of language, not to
the *categories* "language" and "cognition." A careful engagement distinguishes accepting
their model-side pluralism from the further HPC claim about the categories. Their Kepler-
without-Newton analogy (capturing modal structure without mechanism) is also usable against
the draft's own homeostatic-mechanism demand ~-- worth a sentence anticipating that.

order is Ladyman-first here, Nefdt-first on the BBS commentary.
