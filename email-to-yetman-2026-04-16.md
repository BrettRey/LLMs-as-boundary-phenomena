Hi Cameron,

I read your forthcoming *Ergo* paper on representation in LLMs and thought you might be interested in some work I've been doing that converges with yours from a different direction.

I've been applying homeostatic property cluster (HPC) theory from philosophy of science to linguistic and cognitive categories. Two papers connect directly to your argument:

1. **LLMs as boundary phenomena** ([PhilArchive](https://philarchive.org/rec/REYLAB)). This argues that the debate over whether LLMs are "really" cognitive is a category-boundary problem, not a factual one. Your conclusion that LLMs are sometimes representational and sometimes look-up-table-driven is exactly what HPC theory predicts for a boundary case — the properties cluster in core instances and dissociate at the margins.

2. **Reference as a cluster concept: A reply to Coelho Mollo and Milliere** ([PhilArchive](https://philarchive.org/rec/REYRAA-2)). You cite their 2026 paper for content determination. My reply argues that referential grounding is itself a graded cluster property (a "groundedness profile") rather than a binary verdict. The MI techniques you defend could operationalise the individual strands of that profile.
